    RUNE MANIC

    How to play

    Select or drag a rank 1 card to a glowing space. Select or drag a higher-rank card to a glowing rune to upgrade it.

    Use Sort to organize the hand by color or rank. Color sorting groups multicolor runes by their full color combination and orders each group by rank.

    Drag one of your runes to a glowing friendly position to move or swap, or onto a glowing enemy rune to attack. Alternatively, select it and choose Move, Attack or Attribute from its action wheel or the sidebar buttons. Each rune can move once and act once per turn.

    Right-click the game to cancel your current selection.

    On your runes, ○ means available and ✓ means already used this turn.

    Hover over or focus a target to see an arrow: red for attacks and gold for attributes. Selected multi-target runes stay linked until the action resolves.

    Dashed violet arrows show Spectral disabling; solid teal arrows show Amplifying. Arrows point from the source to its target. Select a rune for details.

    A backline rune needs a frontline rune directly ahead. A player with no runes in play loses; if both lose their last runes together, the round is a draw. A locked Spectral stalemate can also draw the round.

    Four consecutive turns without meaningful progress draw the round. Playing, upgrading, damaging, healing or destroying a rune, or changing an attribute status, resets the count; movement alone does not.

    During your turn, offer a draw for the opponent to accept or decline. The AI considers the current board position and declines until both players have completed a turn.

    Occupied columns group together visually. Empty positions appear while moving a rune, or when they are valid places to play a selected card. A rune entering an empty frontline position stays on the selected side as the group re-centers. With three frontline runes, Frontline 5 offers another way to place the fourth rune on the right. It uses the same empty position as the left-hand choice; the four-rune limit still applies. Position labels refer to the original battlefield slots, and each backline stays behind its matching frontline.
